Main Key Insights

  1. Identification of Consistently High-Cost Destinations:

    • Insight: You can instantly see which destination cities are the most expensive to serve, regardless of where the truck is coming from. Look for columns that are predominantly red. This indicates that arriving at this city is inherently costly (e.g., due to congestion taxes, poor unloading facilities, remote location).

    • Example: If the "San Antonio" column is mostly dark red, it's an expensive destination for everyone.

  2. Identification of Consistently High-Cost Origins:

    • Insight: Similarly, you can identify origin cities that are expensive to start from. Look for rows that are predominantly red. This could indicate challenges like getting empty trucks to that location (repositioning cost), or the city being far from major highways.

    • Example: If the "Houston" row is mostly dark red, it's expensive to ship goods out of Houston.

  3. The Asymmetry of Lane Cost:

    • Insight: This is the most critical insight. The cost from A->B is often not the same as from B->A. The heatmap makes this crystal clear.

    • Example: Sacramento -> Los Langeles might be high cost, while Los Langeles-> Sacramento might be yellow (low cost). This almost always points to imbalance.

      • Red (High Cost) Direction: Likely the direction where trucks are full. The high cost could be due to traffic, tolls, or terrain.

      • Green (Low Cost) Direction: Likely the direction where trucks are empty or less full (a "deadhead" or weak backhaul lane). The cost is lower only because you're just counting fuel to return, but the revenue per mile is also likely terrible.

  4. Finding Efficient and Problematic Specific Lanes:

    • Insight: You can pinpoint the exact efficiency of every single origin-destination pair (each cell). This allows you to find hidden gems (efficient lanes) and the worst offenders (very specific problematic lanes).

  5. Network Imbalance Visualization:

    • Insight: The overall pattern of reds and greens across the entire map shows you the flow of your network. It visually answers: "Where are we full? Where are we empty? Where is it cheap to run trucks and where is it expensive?"

Recommended Actions (From Insight to Action)
1. Expensive Destination:

  • Destination Surcharge: Implement a fee for deliveries to this city to cover the higher costs.
  • Appointment Optimization: Work with receivers to secure off-peak delivery times to avoid congestion.
  • Consolidation: Try to consolidate multiple smaller shipments into one full truckload for this destination to improve cost efficiency.

 

2. Expensive Origin

  • Repositioning Strategy: Analyze the cost of positioning empty trucks there. Could you use a cheaper spotter truck or rail?
  • Local Driver Pool: If the origin is remote, consider using local drivers to avoid long repositioning hauls.

 

3. Asymmetrical Lane (A->B is High-Cost, B->A is Low-Cost)

  • Backhaul Prioritization #1: This is the prime opportunity. The green direction (B->A) is your empty backhaul. AGGRESSIVELY search for freight to fill that lane. Even offering a discounted rate will be more profitable than running empty.
  • Pricing Strategy: The price for the red direction (A->B) must be high enough to cover the total cost of the round trip, as the backhaul may be weak.

 

4. Specific Very High-Cost Lane (A Single High-Cost Cell)

  • Root Cause Analysis: Investigate this specific route. Is it a mountain pass? Are there seasonal issues? Are there long detours?
  • Renegotiate or Exit: If the root cause can't be fixed (e.g., a terrible road), you must either renegotiate the price with the customer or consider stopping service on that lane.

 

5. Specific Very Efficient Lane (A Single Low-Cost Cell)

  • Best Practice: Study this lane. Why is it so efficient? Good highway access? No tolls? Perfect load matching?
  • Business Development: Use this efficient, profitable lane as a model. Can you find more customers for this origin or destination?
