The "Inconsistent Miler"

Profile: Efficient but NOT Reliable and NOT Fast

Key Implications and Action Items

  • Substantial Cost-Saving Opportunity: The wide variance indicates that driving behavior, vehicle maintenance, or route selection are not standardized. Bringing underperforming drivers up to the standard of the top performers would result in major fuel savings for the company.

  • Focus on Training: Drivers like Sara Kim and Emma Stone should be the primary focus for targeted training. This training could cover:

    • Eco-driving techniques (smooth acceleration/braking, avoiding excessive idling, proper gear shifting).

    • Route optimization to avoid heavy traffic and unnecessary mileage.

    • Vehicle maintenance awareness (e.g., importance of proper tire pressure, which greatly impacts fuel economy).

  • Investigate Underlying Causes: Before assuming it's solely driver behavior, it's worth checking if external factors are at play:

    • Vehicle Assignment: Do Sara and Emma have older or less fuel-efficient vehicles?

    • Route Difficulty: Are they consistently assigned routes with more stop-and-go traffic or steep gradients?

    • Cargo Load: Do their routes typically involve heavier loads?

  • Recognize and Replicate Good Performance: The company should understand what John Doe and Chris Park are doing correctly. Their best practices should be identified and shared with the rest of the team as a standard operating procedure.

  • Set Performance Benchmarks: Management could set a reasonable fuel cost per mile target (e.g., $0.38/mile) and implement a program to help all drivers achieve it.

    For fair and comprehensive analysis, more context is needed:

  • Vehicle Type: A driver in a large truck will inherently have a higher cost per mile than one in a small van. This chart does not normalize for this.

  • Route Characteristics: Urban vs. highway miles dramatically affect fuel efficiency.

  • Time Period: Is this data from a single week, a month, or a year? A larger sample size is more reliable.

  • Units: While clearly cost, the currency (USD, EUR, etc.) is assumed but not explicitly stated.
